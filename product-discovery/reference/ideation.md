# Ideation toolkit

Use when the user has a vague area of interest, a list of half-ideas, or one idea that needs
widening before it is narrowed. Diverge first, converge second; don't evaluate while
generating.

## 1. Problem storming (15 min)

List 5–10 problems you have **experienced or observed** — in work, health, education,
money, sustainability, daily life. For each, note:

| Problem | Who has it | How often | Intensity (1–5) | Current workaround | Already paying for it? | Our unfair advantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Prefer problems that are **frequent, intense, and already cost money** (painkillers, not
vitamins). "User entrepreneurship" — solving a problem you have yourself — is a strong start,
because you can reach people like you and understand them deeply.

## 2. Founder-fit inventory (Effectuation: Bird-in-Hand)

Before choosing, list your means:

- **Who I am** — skills, traits, interests that will last years.
- **What I know** — domains, industries, technologies, processes.
- **Whom I know** — people who could be customers, partners, advisors or first sellers.
- **Affordable loss** — time and money I can lose without regret.

Ideas that use these assets are faster and cheaper to test.

## 3. How Might We…? (10 min)

Reframe the chosen problem as an opportunity question:

- Start from the user and their need; don't embed the solution.
  - Weak: "How might we build an app for student housing?"
  - Better: "How might we help international students find affordable, safe housing before
    they arrive?"
- Broad enough to allow several solutions, narrow enough to act on.
- Write 3–5 variants (zoom in, zoom out, flip the negative, focus on an emotion).

## 4. Crazy 8s / Crazy 4s (10 min)

Fold a sheet into 8 (or 4) panels; one rough sketch per minute, one idea per panel. Quantity
over quality, wild ideas welcome, stick figures fine, a few labels per sketch. Share and dot-vote.

## 5. SCAMPER (10 min)

Push the best idea through 3 prompts that challenge your assumptions most:

| Prompt | Question |
|---|---|
| **S**ubstitute | What component, material, channel or customer could be replaced? |
| **C**ombine | What could be merged with it — another product, service, or data source? |
| **A**dapt | What could we borrow from another industry or context? |
| **M**odify | What could be magnified, minimised, reshaped, made faster? |
| **P**ut to other use | Who else could use it, or for what other job? |
| **E**liminate | What can be removed while still doing the job? |
| **R**everse | What if we flipped the order, roles, or business model? |

## 6. Rose / Thorn / Bud feedback (1-minute micro-pitch)

Pitch for 60 seconds, then collect:

- **Rose** — what's great?
- **Thorn** — what is the biggest challenge or doubt?
- **Bud / next** — one suggestion to improve or validate.

## 7. Four lenses, one idea (convergence)

| Lens | Question to answer now |
|---|---|
| Lean Startup | What is the smallest version you could test this week? |
| Effectuation | What do you already have (2–3 skills, contacts, resources) to start? |
| Disciplined Entrepreneurship | Who, specifically, is your very first customer? |
| Entrepreneurial Mindset | What action in the next 24 hours moves this forward? |

If an idea can't answer all four, park it. Then move to the discovery loop in `SKILL.md`.

## Agent behaviour when brainstorming

- Generate many options quickly, but **label them as unvalidated** (L0).
- Ask the user about their means (skills, network, budget, time) before ranking ideas —
  founder fit beats abstract market attractiveness at this stage.
- Rank with explicit criteria (frequency, intensity, willingness to pay, reachability,
  founder fit, platform risk), not gut feel.
- End with the first concrete validation step for the top idea (who to talk to, what to ask).
