# Financing and ecosystem — what an early product needs to know

Load this only when the conversation turns to funding, a pitch, or where to build. For a
revenue-first project the default answer is: **fund the discovery loop from affordable loss
and early revenue; raise only when money buys a specific, validated milestone.**

## Match the money to the market

| Path | You gain | You give up | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bootstrapping / customer-funded** | Full control, discipline | Speed; risk of being out-scaled | Most revenue-first products, niches, services, B2B tools |
| **Grants / public programmes** | Non-dilutive money, credibility | Time on applications and reporting; scope constraints | Deep tech, research spin-offs, impact, regional programmes |
| **Crowdfunding (reward)** | Demand validation + pre-sales, no equity | "Fulfilment hell" if unit costs are wrong; public failure | Consumer hardware, creative products |
| **Angels / pre-seed / seed** | Speed, mentoring, network | Equity (dilution), some control | Validated problem, fast-growing market |
| **Venture capital (Series A+)** | Large capital for scale | Significant dilution, board control, growth pressure | Winner-take-all markets with network effects |

Don't use VC fuel for a lifestyle business; don't bootstrap a winner-take-all platform war.
Reward-tier crowdfunding is only safe once the per-unit cost (including shipping and fees)
is known.

## Stages (rough, varies by region and year)

| Stage | Typical sources | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed | Founders, friends & family, angels, micro-VCs, grants | Prototype, first validation |
| Seed | Angels, seed funds, accelerators, equity crowdfunding | Prove people want it; early traction |
| Series A–C | Institutional VCs | Scale a model that already works |
| Exit | Acquisition, IPO | Liquidity |

Check current round sizes for the specific region and sector (e.g. PitchBook, Dealroom,
CB Insights, Atomico *State of European Tech*). They move a lot year to year.

## Instruments

- **SAFE** (Simple Agreement for Future Equity): not debt; no interest or maturity; converts
  into shares at the next priced round, at the better of a **valuation cap** or a
  **discount**. Fast and cheap; founders often underestimate the dilution until conversion.
- **Convertible note**: debt that converts at the next priced round (cap and/or discount);
  accrues interest (often single digits %) and has a maturity date, so repayment risk exists.
- **Priced round**: valuation agreed up front; investors buy preferred shares at a set price.
  Example: €8M pre-money + €2M investment = €10M post-money → investor owns 20%. Slower and
  more expensive legally; sets a valuation "high-water mark" to beat next time.
- *"Valuation is vanity, terms are sanity."* Read liquidation preferences, pro-rata rights,
  board composition and vesting before celebrating a headline valuation.

Instruments and their legal treatment differ by country (e.g. SAFEs are a US template;
European equivalents and tax treatment vary) — get local legal advice.

## How VCs decide (why the pitch looks the way it does)

- Portfolio returns follow a **power law**: a fund needs a few outliers to pay for the many
  failures, so VCs look for products that could become very large — not merely profitable.
- The **team** is the top stated factor; warm introductions convert far better than cold
  emails.
- Sector specialists look at different metrics: SaaS → ARR growth, churn, LTV/CAC; fintech →
  transaction volume, take rate, regulatory moat; biotech → trial data, IP; deep tech →
  technical milestones and manufacturing scale.
- Pitch: crisp one-liner, problem → solution → *why now*, traction over vision, team edge,
  business model and market size, a clear ask tied to milestones.

## Investor profiling (when choosing whom to approach)

For each candidate fund: founding and history, fund size and structure, LPs, partners and
their operating backgrounds, stage and ticket size, sector thesis, notable portfolio and exits,
board-seat policy, co-investors, and what founders say about them. Check fit (stage, sector,
geography, check size) before asking for a meeting.

## Ecosystem context (where to build)

Place matters: funding, talent, regulation, customers and community differ by country.

- **GEM (Global Entrepreneurship Monitor)** — annual global report. Key measures: TEA (share of
  adults starting or running a business < 42 months old), Established Business Ownership,
  business exits, fear of failure, and the **NECI** (National Entrepreneurial Context Index =
  average of 13 Entrepreneurial Framework Conditions scored 0–10, with 5 counted as
  "sufficient"): entrepreneurial finance and access to it, government policy (support; taxes
  and bureaucracy), government programmes, entrepreneurial education (school; post-school),
  R&D transfer, commercial and professional infrastructure, market dynamics, entry burdens and
  regulation, physical infrastructure, social and cultural norms.
  <https://www.gemconsortium.org>
- **EU**: the *EU Startup and Scaleup Strategy — Choose Europe to start and scale*
  (COM(2025) 270, May 2025) targets fragmentation across the single market, access to capital,
  talent and regulatory simplification; the proposed **"28th regime" / EU-Inc** would give
  startups a single EU-wide company form. Check current status before relying on either.
- **Local support**: university tech-transfer offices, incubators and accelerators, national
  innovation agencies and grant programmes (in Austria e.g. aws and FFG; university
  programmes such as the University of Vienna's innovation labs and pre-incorporation seed
  programmes). Ask the user's location before recommending specifics.
- Brad Feld's *Boulder Thesis*: entrepreneurs lead the community, long-term commitment,
  inclusive, continuous engagement; "give first" — mentor and share, it compounds.
