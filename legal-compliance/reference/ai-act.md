# EU AI Act — what a product team must do

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Risk-tiered, extraterritorial (applies if the output is used in
the EU).

**Timeline — verified September 2026.** The high-risk deadlines moved; a lot of guidance
written in 2025 is now wrong. Re-check before relying on any date here.

| Obligation | Applies from |
| --- | --- |
| Art. 5 prohibited practices | 2 Feb 2025 (in force) |
| Art. 4 AI-literacy duty | 2 Feb 2025 (in force; substance softened by the Omnibus) |
| General-purpose AI model obligations | 2 Aug 2025 (in force) |
| Art. 50 transparency (disclose AI, label synthetic content) | 2 Aug 2026 (in force) |
| Annex III high-risk — standalone systems | **2 Dec 2027** (deferred from 2 Aug 2026) |
| Annex I high-risk — AI embedded in regulated products | **2 Aug 2028** (deferred) |
| New prohibition: non-consensual intimate imagery / CSAM generation | 2 Dec 2026 |

The deferral came from the Digital Omnibus amending regulation, in force 27 Jul 2026. It
delays the high-risk *deadlines*; it does not remove the obligations. Building to the
original schedule is still the safe engineering choice — the extra time is for conformity
assessment and standards that were not ready, not a signal that requirements will shrink.

## Which tier are you in?

```
Does the system do any of these?  → PROHIBITED (Art. 5)
  social scoring by public authorities · exploiting vulnerabilities of children/disabled ·
  emotion inference at work or in education · untargeted facial-image scraping ·
  biometric categorisation for sensitive traits · predictive policing on personality
      → do not build it
Is it used for one of Annex III purposes? → HIGH RISK
  employment/recruitment/promotion decisions · access to education or exam scoring ·
  essential services eligibility · law enforcement · biometrics · critical infrastructure
      → conformity assessment, risk management, data governance, logging, human oversight,
        accuracy/robustness testing, technical documentation, registration
Does it interact with people, or generate/manipulate content? → TRANSPARENCY (Art. 50)
      → tell users they are dealing with AI; label synthetic content and deepfakes
Otherwise → MINIMAL
      → voluntary codes of conduct; still meet GDPR
```

## Traps for assessment / scoring apps

- **Emotion recognition in workplace and education is prohibited.** Inferring emotional
  state from voice, face or text in those contexts is out — a self-report questionnaire
  scored by fixed arithmetic is not an emotion-recognition AI system at all.
- **Deterministic scoring is not AI.** A published scoring key applied with arithmetic falls
  outside the AI Act. Adding an LLM that writes the interpretation *does* bring the AI Act's
  transparency duties, and GDPR Art. 22 if the output drives decisions.
- **Selection decisions are high risk.** If a profile is used to pick who gets into an
  school, a job, or a benefit, you are close to or inside Annex III. Keep a human decision
  maker who can and does override, and record that they did.
- **Art. 4 AI literacy**: staff operating an AI system must be trained. Cheap to satisfy,
  easy to forget — write a one-page internal note and log who read it.

## Minimum implementation for an LLM feature

1. **Disclosure** in the UI at the point of output: "This summary was generated by AI and
   reviewed by a professional."
2. **Human in the loop** for anything affecting a person: draft state → professional
   approves → then visible to the subject.
3. **Logging**: model, version, prompt template id, timestamp, input hash, output, reviewer.
4. **Data governance**: do not send special-category data to a model provider without a DPA
   and a lawful basis; prefer pseudonymised inputs.
5. **Accuracy statement**: describe known limitations in the policy and the UI.

## GDPR Art. 22 interaction

A decision based solely on automated processing with legal or similarly significant effect
is prohibited unless consent, contract necessity, or law applies — and even then the subject
gets the right to human intervention, to express a view, and to contest. State in the policy
whether automated decision-making occurs; if it does not, say so.

## Documentation to keep

- System description, purpose, tier classification and the reasoning.
- Data sources and their lawful basis.
- Evaluation results and known failure modes.
- Human oversight procedure and who performs it.
- Incident log.
